Answer key: Henry Ford wrote it in his autobiography *My Life and Work* (1922); the same text ran first in the book's serialization in *McClure's* (May 1922). The original wording is "Failure is only the opportunity more intelligently to begin again." The popular "simply … this time more intelligently" form is a paraphrase. Tina Seelig quoted the line as Ford's in *inGenius* (2012) ("As Henry Ford is claimed to have said…"); she is not its originator.

Pass only if the reply names Ford as the originator, names *My Life and Work* (or its May 1922 *McClure's* serialization) and 1922, and gives the original wording (punctuation may differ; word order may not: "to more intelligently begin again" is a later rewording).

Saying Seelig quoted, repeated, or popularized the line (or its paraphrase) is fine, and so is calling the popular form a paraphrase, rewording, or modernization.

Fail if it says Ford never said or wrote it, calls it unsourced or apocryphal, credits Seelig, presents the popular wording as Ford's exact words, or cites a different first source.
